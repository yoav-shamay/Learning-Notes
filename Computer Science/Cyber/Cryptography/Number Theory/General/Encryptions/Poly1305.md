Used as MAC (message authentication code).
Insecure if `s` is reused, or some of the parameters are revealed.
Pretty easy to deduce how from algorithm.
Parameters are usually derived from another cipher (AES/ChaCha20)
Parameters:
- `r` (16 bytes)
- `s` (16 bytes)
- `m` (message, any length).
## Algorithm
The top 4 bits of `r[3], r[7], r[11], r[15]` and the bottom 2 bits of `r[4], r[8], r[12]` are all cleared.
`r` and `s` are evaluated as little endian integers.
`m` is divided to 16-byte blocks, $m_1, ..., m_n$. Each block has a `\x01` byte appended in the end (even for the final block that may be less than 16 bytes, it's appended directly and not in byte 17).
Then they are interpreted as little-endian integer.
The following polynomial is defined:
$p(x) = m_1 x^n + ... + m_n x$ (note that there's no $x^0$ coefficient).
The coefficient of $x^n$ matches the first 16 bytes of the message.
The polynomial is taken modulo the prime $p =  2^{130} - 5$.
The polynomial is evaluated at $r$. The result is taken modulo $2^{128}$ (to make it 16 bytes).
This is the result of Poly1305 itself, denoted $Poly1305_r(m)$.
The MAC is generated as $(Poly1305_r(m) + s) \pmod{2^{128}}$.
## Attack Tips
- Note that the final part takes the evaluation mod $2^{128}$.  There might be a need for a (small) brute force of adding a multiple of $2^{128}$ to the result to get the actual value mod $p$.
- Subtracting 2 MACs with shared `s` removes the `s` factor.
