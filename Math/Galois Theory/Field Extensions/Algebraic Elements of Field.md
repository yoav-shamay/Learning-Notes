## Algebraic and Transcendent Elements
In a field extension $K/F$.
An element $a \in K$ is called algebraic iff there exists $p(x) \in F[x]$ such that $f(a) = 0$.
- $a \in K$ is algebraic iff $[F(a) : F]$ is finite, iff $F[a] = F(a)$
## Extension creating root of a polynomial
Given an irreducible polynomial $p(x) \in F[x]$, there exists an extension $K/F$ where $p(x)$ has a root.
The extension is $F[x] / (f(x))$ (the ideal generated by $f(x)$).
It's isomorphic to $F(a)$ where $a$ denotes the root, and it's the element matching $x$ in the division ring. $a$ is an algebraic element.
Therefore for any polynomial $p(x) \in F[x]$, there exists a finite extension of $F$ where $p(x)$ factors to linear factors.
## Minimal Polynomial of an element
Given algebraic $a \in K$ in a field extension $K / F$, the monomial $p(x) \in F[x]$ with minimum degree with $a$ as root is called the minimal polynomial of $a$ over $F$.
If the degree of $p(x)$ is $n$, $a$ is called algebraic of degree $n$ over $F$.
- The degree is always at least 1, it's 1 iff $a \in F$.
- $[F(a) : F] = n$, where $n$ is the degree of the minimal polynomial.
## Equivalence over isomorphism
If $F \cong \overline{F}$, $f(x)$ irreducible over $F$, and $\overline{f}(x)$ the matching polynomial in $\overline{F}$.
Denote $a$ and $\overline{a}$ roots of $f(x)$ and $\overline{f}(x)$ in extensions $K/F$ and $\overline{K}/\overline{F}$.
Then $F(a)$ is isomorphic to $\overline{F}(a)$, with an isomorphism that it's reduction to $F$ is the same original isomorphism between $F$ and $\overline{F}$.
