For $f$ defined and continuous on $[a, b]$, for each $\varepsilon > 0$, there exists a polynomial $P(x)$ such that $|P(x) - f(x)| < \varepsilon$ for all $x$ in $[a, b]$.
Alternatively, there's a sequence of polynomials that converges uniformly to $f$ on $[a, b]$.
This means that you can approximate every function on a closed range arbitrarily precisely with a polynomial.

See [[Bernstein polynomial]] for a construction.
Alternatively, [[Fourier Series#Fejer Sums]] can provide a trigonometric polynomial approximation to $f(\cos x)$, which privides a polynomial approximation to $f(x)$ (on $[-1, 1]$, can be easily shifted).
