 Some of this assumes the characteristic of the field isn't 2.
Appears in [[Bilinear Forms]] and [[Quadratic Forms]] base changing.
$A$ is conjugate to $B$ if there's an invertible matrix $M$ satisfying $M^tAM = B$.
## Properties
- if $A$ is symmetric and $B$ is conjugate to $A$ then $A$ is symmetric
- Conjugate matrices has the same rank
## Diagonalization
- Every symmetric matrix is conjugate to a diagonal matrix
- Therefore, every bilinear form can be represented as $\sum a_ix_iy_i$ and every quadratic form as $\sum a_i x_i^2$
- Note that orthogonal diagonalization is a special case on $\mathbb{R}$.
- See [[Quadratic Forms#Lagrange method to representing as sum of matrices]] for a method to perform - just assume it's a quadratic form.
- See [[Elementary Conjugate]] for an alternative algorithm.
- Can also be diagonalized using [[Unitary Diagnoalization]] on the reals
- The diagonal form isn't unique - $diag(\delta_1,...,\delta_n)$ commutes with $diag(\gamma_1^2\delta_1,...,\gamma_n^2\delta_n)$ for all $\gamma_1,...,\gamma_n$.
