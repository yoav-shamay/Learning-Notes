For a self-adjoint transformation $T: V \rightarrow V$:
- If for every $u \in V$, $(Tu, u) \geq 0$ then T is called non-negative (or positive-semidefinite)
- if $T$ is non-negative and $(Tu, u) = 0 \iff u = 0$ then $T$ is called (positive-definite)
- negative-semidefinite and negative-definite is similarly defined (and have similar theorems)
- בעברית: אי-שלילית, חיובית לחלוטין (ואי-חיובית, שלילית לחלוטין)
- T is positive definite iff for an orthonormal basis $B$, $[T]_B$ is a positive definite matrix.
- A positive definite matrix is hermitian and satisfying $\sum_{i=1}^n\sum_{j=1}^n A_{ij} x_i \overline x_j > 0$ for every scalars $x_1, ..., x_n$ such that at least one is non-zero.
- $(Tu, v)$ is an inner product iff $T$ is positive definite.
- All eigenvalues of a non-negative (positive-definite) transformation are non-negative (positive)
- A hermitian matrix is positive definite iff all of its eigenvalues are positive reals
